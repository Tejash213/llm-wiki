## Summary
A recurring pattern in public reaction to new technologies where plausible concerns escalate into exaggerated doomsday narratives; historical examples include overpopulation, peak oil, and other high-profile predictions that did not unfold as feared.

## Key Points
- Public fears about new technologies often follow a pattern: an observable risk is amplified into a broad existential threat.
- Historical examples (e.g., 1970s overpopulation, peak oil) show that early extreme predictions frequently miss adaptive responses and technological change.
- Understanding this pattern helps balance genuine concern with perspective when evaluating contemporary tech risks like AI.
